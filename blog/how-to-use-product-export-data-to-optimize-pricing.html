How to Use Product Export Data to Optimize Your Pricing Strategy

Pricing is one of the most powerful levers in e-commerce. A price set too high will kill your conversion rates; a price set too low will leave significant profit margins on the table. To design a pricing strategy that drives sales while maximizing profit margins, you must base your decisions on solid competitor data, not assumptions.

In this guide, we'll explain how to use competitor product export spreadsheets to audit market rates, establish retail markdowns, and optimize variant pricing.

Step 1: Collect Competitor Pricing Data

Begin by selecting your top 3 competitors and exporting their catalogs using Shopify Product Exporter. Save these sheets as Excel workbooks. Focus specifically on the *Variant Price* and *Variant Compare At Price* columns, which list the active sale prices and the original retail prices.

Step 2: Calculate Average Market Values

Combine the competitor datasets in Google Sheets or Excel. Use formula functions to find the pricing parameters for your overlapping products:

  • `=AVERAGE(price_range)`: Calculates the average market price, establishing your baseline.
  • `=MIN(price_range)`: Identifies the lowest price in the market. Useful if you want to compete on a low-cost volume model.
  • `=MAX(price_range)`: Shows the premium ceiling, indicating the maximum consumers are currently willing to pay.

Step 3: Analyze Markdown and Discounting Patterns

Review the compare-at price columns. Calculate the average discount percentage they offer across their catalog:

`Discount % = (Compare At Price - Price) / Compare At Price`

If your competitors consistently offer discounts of 30% to 40% using compare-at prices, consumers in your niche expect this markup structure. To compete, price your products similarly with a similar compare-at markup to make your listings feel valuable.

Step 4: Check for Variant Price Premiums

Inspect variant pricing. Do they charge more for specific variants (e.g. charging an extra $3 for an XL shirt compared to a Small, or charging more for popular color options)? Mappings variants separately reveals where you can optimize your margins by applying premium pricing to high-demand options.

Conclusion

A data-driven pricing model is a necessity for modern e-commerce. By exporting competitor catalogs with **Shopify Product Exporter** and auditing their pricing structures in Excel, you secure the data required to optimize your margins and increase conversion rates.
